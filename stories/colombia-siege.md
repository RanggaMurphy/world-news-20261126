# Dossier 5 — Colombia supermarket siege

Story 5 of 36. Grid. Region: South America. Pin: Cartagena,
Colombia. Certainty: CONFIRMED.

## Summary

Colombian police stormed a supermarket in Cartagena and freed 76
people held for 17 hours. One gunman was killed. The president said
a robbery of a cash-laden armoured car had gone wrong. The hostage
count of 76 is the number the page carries in the numbers strip.

## Verified facts

- The siege took place in a supermarket in Cartagena.
- 76 people were held hostage.
- They were held for 17 hours.
- Police stormed the supermarket and freed them.
- One gunman was killed in the storming.
- The president said a robbery had gone wrong.
- The robbery involved a cash-laden armoured car.
- Early reports carried lower hostage counts; the police figure
  of 76 is the one the desk prints.

## In one sentence

Police stormed a supermarket in Cartagena and freed 76 people held
for 17 hours, killing one gunman, after a robbery of a cash-laden
armoured car went wrong.

## Weighing the reporting

Five outlets carried the story: BBC, ABC, NBC, Reuters, Le Monde.
The hostage count moved during the day, and the desk waited for the
police figure of 76 before locking the numbers strip. The president's
account, that a robbery went wrong, is the only explanation of
motive on the record. No outlet has yet confirmed how the gunmen got
into the supermarket, how many gunmen there were beyond the one
killed, or whether anyone else was hurt in the storming.

What is still unknown: the full number of attackers, the fate of any
surviving gunmen, the condition of the freed hostages, and whether
the armoured car's cash was recovered. The desk watches for the
police report, which will settle the count of attackers and the
question of whether this was a robbery that escalated or a siege
that was planned.

The scene on the page is a drawing of a shopfront with a police
shield in front of it. It illustrates the category of the story. It
does not show the real supermarket and it does not show the
hostages.

## Sources in detail

Five outlets carried the story: BBC, ABC, NBC, Reuters, Le Monde.
The BBC carried the storming and the hostage count. ABC and NBC
carried the 17 hours and the freed hostages. Reuters carried the
police figure of 76 and the president's account of the robbery.
Le Monde carried the Cartagena setting and the armoured car. The
hostage count moved during the day, and the desk waited for the
police figure before locking the numbers strip. All five agree on
the 76, the 17 hours, and the one gunman killed.

## What the desk watches next

The police report, which will settle the count of attackers and
whether any survived. The condition of the freed hostages. Whether
the armoured car's cash was recovered. Whether the robbery was a
plan that escalated or a siege that was planned from the start. The
