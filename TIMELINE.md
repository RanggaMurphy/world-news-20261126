# Timeline — Thursday 26 November 2026, in UTC

This file orders the day hour by hour in UTC, so a reader can see
when each story happened and how the day unfolded. Times are the
desk's best reading of the reporting; where a story was reported
during the day without a fixed hour, it is placed in the band the
reporting supports.

## 00:00 to 03:00 UTC

**02:00 — Solomon Islands signs the Australia pact.** The treaty is
signed in Canberra, making Australia the islands' partner of choice
and ruling out any foreign military base. The funding package is
740 million dollars over a decade. This is the first stamp on the
page's UTC strip, and the earliest timed event of the day.

Overnight in the same band, Ukrainian drones hit the Syzran and
Kuibyshev refineries in Samara and the Novoshakhtinsk refinery in
Rostov. One person is killed in Samara. The strikes are reported
in the morning in Europe.

## 03:00 to 06:00 UTC

**05:00 — Houthis claim the Ras Tanura strike.** The Houthi
spokesman announces strikes on the Ras Tanura refinery and Riyadh's
financial district. Satellite pictures show a wide smoke plume.
Riyadh says four drones were downed and does not confirm a direct
hit. Brent crude climbs past 94 dollars.

In the same band, the Revolutionary Guard claims two tankers hit
mines in the Gulf early on Wednesday. The claim stays unverified
through the day.

## 06:00 to 09:00 UTC

**07:00 — Cairo fire toll confirmed at 31.** The health ministry's
figure lands: 31 dead, 21 injured, after a fuel tanker overturned
on the ring road and burst into flames. The toll is the number the
page carries in its numbers strip.

Morning in Asia: Taiwan's market opens to a rout. The index falls
1,927 points on the day, its sixth-largest points fall, and the
currency touches its lowest in about nineteen months.

## 09:00 to 12:00 UTC

Midday in Europe: the General Court rules that the Commission
failed the End the Cage Age initiative. About 300 million animals
are still caged. A proposal on hens is expected on 9 December.

In the same band, India's cabinet approves five items worth 19,934
crore rupees, and the IITs announce 6,000 new seats in robotics,
AI, and space from 2027. India marks Constitution Day and the 18th
anniversary of the Mumbai attacks.

## 12:00 to 15:00 UTC

Afternoon in Europe: the Champions League results from Wednesday
night settle into the morning papers. Liverpool 3-0 at Club Brugge,
Barcelona 4-1 at Sabah, PSG held 2-2 by Roma, Bayern beaten 2-1 at
Lille. Real Madrid are now the only perfect side left.

In South Asia, the IPL auction register is published: 1,220 players
for 66 slots, the auction set for 16 December in Goa.

## 15:00 to 18:00 UTC

**18:56 — S and P puts Nvidia on watch.** The rating agency places
Nvidia's AA credit rating on watch for a cut, citing governance
risks. A one-notch cut is the likely outcome of a 90-day review.
Nvidia shares fall about 4 percent.

In the same band, North Korea orders the Swiss office in Pyongyang
shut and its head out within 48 hours, and the United States trade
office's tariff proposal on 14 economies is published.

## 18:00 to 21:00 UTC

**20:18 — Federal Reserve Beige Book released.** Eight of twelve
districts report slight to moderate growth, four are flat.
Employment rises slightly, wages and prices rise moderately.

**21:00 — Wall Street closes after the comeback.** The Dow erases a
loss of more than 1,100 points and closes up 0.33 percent at
50,183. The S and P and the Nasdaq finish lower. Thanksgiving
closure follows the next day.

## 21:00 to 24:00 UTC

**22:33 — Andy Taylor death announced.** The family and the band
announce that the Duran Duran guitarist died at home on Monday,
aged 65, of metastatic prostate cancer. It is the last stamp on
the page's UTC strip.

Late in the band, the Colombia siege ends: police storm the
supermarket in Cartagena and free 76 people held for 17 hours.
One gunman is killed. The siege is the day's longest-running story
by the clock, and it closes the day's news in the Americas.

## The day in one line

The day opens with a treaty in Canberra, breaks with a claim in the
Gulf and a fire in Cairo, falls with a market rout in Taiwan and on
Wall Street, and closes with a comeback in New York and a siege
ending in Cartagena. Seven UTC stamps on the page mark that arc.

## How the desk ordered the day

The timeline above is ordered by UTC hour, but the desk made
judgement calls that a reader should know about. The Champions
League results were played on Wednesday 25 November in local time
and reported that night; they are placed in the 12:00 to 15:00 UTC
band because that is when they became the morning sport story of
the 26th in European time zones. The F-15EX public display is placed
on the 26th for the same reason: the jets arrived on 15 November,
but the display, which is the story, was on the 25th in local time
and reported as the day's news on the 26th.

The Andy Taylor announcement is placed at 22:33 UTC because that is
the timestamp on the wire that carried it. The death itself was on
Monday 23 November. The desk counts the first report date as the
edition date, and the announcement is the first report. The same
rule puts the estate filing, story 36, on the page and leaves the
16 August death off it.

Stories without a fixed hour, the Gaza strike, the Colombia siege,
the Bangladesh decision, are placed in the band the reporting
supports rather than given a false precision. The desk would rather
place a story in a three-hour band honestly than invent an hour.
