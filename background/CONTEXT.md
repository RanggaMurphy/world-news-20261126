together the stories the page separates into tiers, and explains
the pattern the page only implies.

## How to use them

Read the page first. Then read the briefing for the theme that
interested you. The briefings do not change any label on the page.
They explain why the labels are what they are, what is still
unknown, and what the desk watches next.

## What they are not

They are not news. They are the desk's reading of the news of one
day, written in the same session as the page, from the same sources,
paraphrased throughout. Where a briefing offers an interpretation,
it says so. Where it states a fact, the fact is traceable to
`sources.json` and to the dossier of the story it belongs to.

## One line per briefing

- **Energy shock.** The day was a day of claims, and claims need
  more room than a headline gives them.
- **Markets and the AI question.** Five market stories are one
  story: the AI trade under pressure, the economy still growing,
  and nobody sure which one is right.
- **The tariff proposal.** A negotiation in numbers: fourteen caps
  and three fallbacks, and who is being threatened and who is being
  offered a deal.
- **South Asia on the day.** A region raising wages, building
  ports, training engineers, and sending its young people to records
  and to space.
- **The Pacific shift.** A region choosing sides, one signature and
  one posting at a time.
- **Europe and its courts.** A day made of institutions doing their
  work: a court, a competition, a championship, and a band in
  mourning.
