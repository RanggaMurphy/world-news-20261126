# Background briefing — Markets and the AI question

How the day's market stories fit together: a comeback on Wall
Street, a rating watch on Nvidia, a rout in Taiwan, a warning about
an AI bust, and a Federal Reserve survey that says the economy is
still growing. Read this before the dossiers for stories 3 and 9,
and the briefs for stories 14, 15, and 34.

## The comeback

The Dow fell more than 1,100 points in the morning and closed up
0.33 percent at 50,183. The S and P closed down 0.25 percent at
7,183. The Nasdaq closed down 0.62 percent at 23,586. Nvidia fell
about 4 percent. The morning lows were about 4 percent down on the
Nasdaq and about 3 percent on the S and P. The drivers were an AI
selloff, tariff fears, and hot inflation data.

The comeback is the story because of its size. A 1,100-point swing
intraday, erased by the close, is not a normal day. It is the kind
of day that gets remembered, and it is the kind of day that can mean
two opposite things: a market that found its footing, or a market
that is about to fall harder. The desk does not choose between those
readings. It prints the numbers and lets the reader decide.

## The rating watch

S and P placed Nvidia's AA credit rating on watch for a cut, citing
governance risks after the chief's exit and return. A one-notch cut
is the likely outcome of a 90-day review. The chief resigned on 11
November and was reinstated on 22 November. Moody's kept its Aa1
rating, with the outlook moved to stable from positive on 18
November. United States officials told Congress there was no Chinese
involvement in the chief's affairs.

The rating watch matters for two reasons. First, Nvidia is the
company the AI boom is built on, and a rating action against it is a
signal that the boom has a governance problem, not just a valuation
problem. Second, the two agencies disagree: S and P is worried,
Moody's is not. When the agencies disagree, the market has to
choose, and the choice will move the price.

## The rout in Taiwan

Taiwan's index fell 1,927 points, about 4.8 percent, its
sixth-largest points fall on record, with an intraday drop of 3,440
points. The currency touched its lowest in about nineteen months,
and foreign selling ran to 123.654 billion New Taiwan dollars. The
Philadelphia semiconductor index fell 7.88 percent.

Taiwan is where the AI boom is manufactured. A rout there is not a
local story; it is the supply chain of the AI trade falling with the
shares. The desk prints the Taiwan fall as a brief, but it belongs
in this briefing because it is the same story as the Wall Street
rout and the Nvidia watch: the AI trade, all at once, under pressure.

## The warning

Economists warned that a fall in AI investment could hit harder
than the dot-com bust. Barclays estimates AI investment drove a
quarter of United States growth last year, and data-centre building
added half a point of GDP. Stocks now make up 35 percent of
household net worth, against 26 percent at the dot-com peak. Some
economists say the fallout could exceed the dot-com bust, when the
Nasdaq fell about 80 percent.

The warning is an opinion built on estimates, which is why it is
labelled REPORTED. But the numbers behind it are real: a quarter of
growth from one sector, a third of household wealth in stocks. If
the AI trade is a bubble, it is a bigger bubble than the dot-com
one, and it is holding up more of the economy.

## The survey that says growth

The Beige Book reported slight to moderate growth in eight of
twelve districts and flat conditions in four. Employment rose
slightly, wages and benefits rose moderately, prices rose moderately.
Third-quarter GDP was revised to 4.3 percent annualised, the fastest
in three years. A December rate hike is near fully priced, and the
Federal Reserve meets on 8 to 9 December.

The survey is the counterweight to the market stories. The economy
is growing at 4.3 percent while the market falls. That gap is the
day's real question: is the market wrong about the economy, or is
the economy about to catch up with the market? The desk does not
answer it. It prints both sides.

## What to watch

- The next market open after Thanksgiving. A comeback that holds
  is a recovery; a comeback that fails is a dead-cat bounce.
- The 90-day Nvidia review and the next quarterly results.
- The December Federal Reserve meeting on 8 to 9 December, and
  whether the hike that is priced in actually happens.
- Taiwan's market and currency, which will show first whether the
  AI selloff is over or just pausing.

## Why this briefing exists

The page has room for one market story as a feature and one as a
grid item, plus two briefs. The truth about markets on 26 November
2026 is that five stories are one story: the AI trade under
pressure, the economy still growing, and nobody sure which one is
right. This briefing is where that one story is told.

## The week before

The market stories of the 26th did not start on the 26th either.
The AI selloff had been building for weeks, and Nvidia's chief
resigned on 11 November and was reinstated on 22 November, eleven
days of uncertainty that the rating watch is really about. Moody's
moved its outlook to stable from positive on 18 November, a week
before the S and P watch. The tariff fears that drove the morning
rout had been building since the trade office's proposal took shape,
and the hot inflation reading that morning was the trigger, not the
cause.

The desk notes this because the comeback looks like a single day's
story, and it is not. It is a day in a month of pressure on the AI
trade, and the pressure is the story. The page records the day; this
briefing records the month.

## How the theme appears on the page

Markets are the third feature, the Wall Street comeback, and one
grid item, the Nvidia rating watch, plus three briefs: Taiwan, the
Beige Book, and the AI bust warning. The numbers strip carries
Taiwan's 1,927 points and US GDP at 4.3 percent. The UTC strip
carries the Nvidia watch at 18:56, the Beige Book at 20:18, and the
Wall Street close at 21:00. The map carries the New York and Santa
Clara pins. A reader who reads only the page will learn that the
market fell and came back, that Nvidia is on watch, and that Taiwan
fell hard. A reader who reads this briefing will learn that all
five are one story, and that the economy and the market disagree
about the future.
