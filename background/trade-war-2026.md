# Background briefing — The tariff proposal

How the day's trade story fits together: a Section 301 proposal
covering 14 economies, a separate duty on Brazilian aircraft, and
an Indian cabinet package that is the other side of the same coin.
Read this before the dossier for story 8 and the brief for story 35.

## The proposal

The United States trade office proposed Section 301 duties on 14
economies over excess factory capacity. The list is Bangladesh,
Cambodia, China, the European Union, India, Indonesia, Japan,
Korea, Malaysia, Singapore, Switzerland, Taiwan, Thailand, and
Vietnam. Comments are due 5 January 2027 and a hearing is set for
25 January 2027.

The caps differ by economy, and the differences are the story. The
European Union, Japan, Korea, Switzerland, and Taiwan face 15
percent. Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand, and Bangladesh
face 18 percent. China faces 20 percent, with a 40 percent fallback
if the truce fails. Vietnam faces 20 percent, with a 35 percent
fallback. India faces 17 percent, with a 25 percent fallback.

The pattern is deliberate. The lowest caps go to allies and to the
economies the United States needs most. The highest fallbacks go to
the two economies the proposal is really about: China and Vietnam.
The fallback is the threat, and the cap is the offer. The proposal
is a negotiation dressed as a tariff.

## The aircraft duty

A separate 10 percent duty on Brazilian Embraer aircraft was
proposed in the same notice. It is a small item next to the 14
economies, but it matters because it shows the proposal is not only
about Asia. Embraer competes with Boeing, and a duty on its aircraft
is a favour to the domestic manufacturer dressed as trade policy.
The desk records it here because the page has no room for it.

## The legal basis

Section 301 is the United States' tool for acting against foreign
trade practices it calls unfair. The proposal says the 14 economies
have excess factory capacity, which is a polite way of saying they
make too much of what the United States also makes, and sell it too
cheaply. The comment period and the hearing are the formal steps
before any duty is imposed. Nothing is decided yet, and the desk's
label is OFFICIAL because the proposal is an on-record document, not
because the duties are certain.

## The responses to watch

The proposal will draw responses from all 14 economies, and the
responses will differ. Taiwan and Korea, capped at 15 percent, have
the most to lose from a breakdown and the most reason to negotiate.
China, facing 20 percent and a 40 percent fallback, has the least
reason to concede and the most reason to retaliate. The European
Union, capped at 15 percent, will answer as a bloc, and its answer
will set the tone for the others. India, at 17 percent with a 25
percent fallback, sits in the middle: big enough to matter, small
enough to be squeezed.

## The other side of the coin

On the same day, India's cabinet approved five items worth 19,934
crore rupees, including an 11,000-crore construction equipment
scheme, a second terminal at Kamarajar Port, a greenfield airport at
Silchar, and postal schemes. The package is domestic investment, not
a response to the tariff proposal, but it reads as one: a government
building its own capacity while another government taxes it. The two
stories belong together, and the page carries both, one as a grid
item and one as a brief.

## What to watch

- The comments due 5 January 2027, which will show who is
  negotiating and who is fighting.
- The hearing on 25 January 2027, which will show how serious the
  trade office is about the caps.
- The responses from Taipei, Beijing, and Brussels, in that order.
- Whether the truce with China holds. If it fails, the 40 percent
  fallback becomes the story.

## Why this briefing exists

The page has room for the headline count of 14 economies and two
caps. The truth about the proposal is in the table of caps and
fallbacks, which is a negotiation in numbers. This briefing is where
that table is explained, and where the reader can see who is being
threatened and who is being offered a deal.

## The week before

The tariff proposal did not come from nowhere. The trade office
has been building the Section 301 case for months, and the truce
with China, which the 40 percent fallback is tied to, has been
fragile since it was agreed. The 14 economies on the list are the
ones the United States buys the most manufactured goods from, and
the caps reflect how much leverage Washington thinks it has over
each of them. The proposal is the formal step; the negotiation
started long before it.

The desk notes this because a reader who sees only the proposal
will think it is a decision. It is not a decision. It is an opening
bid, and the comment period to 5 January 2027 and the hearing on
25 January 2027 are the next moves. The page records the bid; this
briefing records the game.

## How the theme appears on the page

Trade is one grid item, the tariff proposal, and one brief, the
India cabinet package. The numbers strip does not carry a trade
figure, because the proposal has no single number: it has fourteen
caps and three fallbacks. The UTC strip does not carry it either,
because a proposal has no hour. The map carries the Washington pin.
A reader who reads only the page will learn that Washington
proposed duties on 14 economies. A reader who reads this briefing
will learn that the proposal is a negotiation in numbers, and who
is being threatened and who is being offered a deal.
