# Background briefing — Europe and its courts

How the day's European stories fit together: a court ruling on
caged animals, a night of Champions League football, a chess match
in Geneva, and a guitarist's death. Read this before the dossiers
for stories 10 and 11, and the briefs for stories 25, 28, and 33.

## The ruling

The General Court ruled that the Commission failed the End the Cage
Age initiative, which gathered 1.4 million signatures in 2020 and
was promised action by the end of 2023. About 300 million animals
are still caged, and a proposal on hens is expected on 9 December.

The ruling is about a promise, not about cages. The Commission
promised to propose a ban, and then did not propose it. The court
said that failure was unlawful. The ruling does not free a single
animal, but it forces the Commission to act, and the hens proposal
expected on 9 December is the first test of whether the ruling has
teeth. The desk prints it as a brief and records it here because a
court ruling about a broken promise is the kind of story that
matters more in a month than it does today.

## The football

Matchday 5 of the Champions League, played on Wednesday 25
November: Liverpool 3-0 at Club Brugge, Barcelona 4-1 at Sabah,
Paris held 2-2 by Roma, Bayern beaten 2-1 at Lille, Sporting 2-2
with Manchester United, Inter 2-0 over Stuttgart, Atletico 2-0 over
Viking, Shakhtar 0-1 to Fenerbahce, Slavia 0-1 to Villarreal. Real
Madrid are now the only perfect side left.

The night ended two perfect records: Paris's and Bayern's. That is
why the desk put two football stories in the grid rather than one:
the night had two endings, and one story could not carry both. The
other results are listed in the dossier for story 11, so that the
night is on the record even though the page has room for only two
matches.

## The chess match

Gukesh and Sindarov drew the first two games of their Geneva title
match, 38 moves then 20 moves, leaving the score at 1-1. Game three
falls on Thursday 26 November, with Sindarov playing white. The
match is fourteen classical games, first to 7.5 points, and both
players are twenty.

Geneva is a neutral ground for a title match, and the choice of city
is part of the story: chess has always preferred neutral ground for
its biggest matches. Two twenty-year-olds playing for the world
title is the future of the game arriving on schedule, and the desk
prints it as a brief because the match will be decided over the
next two weeks, not today.

## The guitarist

Duran Duran guitarist Andy Taylor died at home on Monday 23
November, aged 65, of metastatic prostate cancer. His family and
the band announced it on Wednesday 25 November. The announcement
is the story's date, which is why the brief belongs to this
edition and the death does not appear as a 23 November story.

The desk prints the announcement with the same care as the rest of
the page: the date of death, the cause, the age, and the fact that
the family and the band announced it together. Obituaries are the
page's quietest stories, and they deserve the same accuracy as the
loudest ones.

## The excluded ruling

One European story was researched and left out: the Norway court

footer as an exclusion. The desk records it here because it is the
counterpart of the cage ruling: two courts, two rulings about
government promises, one from this week and one from last week.
Only the first belongs on this page.

## Why this briefing exists

The page carries five European stories, and they do not look like
one story: a court, a football night, a chess match, and a death.
They are one story in the sense that matters: Europe's day was
made of institutions doing their work, a court ruling on a promise,
a competition deciding its table, a championship being played, and
a band mourning its guitarist. This briefing is where that day is
told as one day.

## The week before

The European stories of the 26th did not start on the 26th. The
End the Cage Age initiative gathered 1.4 million signatures in
2020, and the Commission promised action by the end of 2023. The
promise was broken for three years before the court ruled on it.
The Champions League table had been forming for four matchdays
before the fifth ended two perfect records. The chess match in
Geneva is the latest in a rivalry that has been building all year.
Andy Taylor had been ill for years before his death on 23 November.

The desk notes this because the page makes the day look like a
series of events, and it is really a series of endings: a promise
kept or broken, a perfect record ended, a match begun, a life ended.
The page records the day; this briefing records what ended.
