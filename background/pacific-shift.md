# Background briefing — The Pacific shift

How the day's Pacific stories fit together: a treaty in Canberra,
jets in Okinawa, a space station over Beijing, and an envoy expelled
from Pyongyang. Read this before the dossiers for stories 12 and
13, and the briefs for stories 16 and 20.

## The treaty

Solomon Islands signed a security treaty making Australia its
partner of choice and ruling out any foreign base. The funding is
740 million dollars over a decade: 670 million in grants and 70
million in loans, for policing, infrastructure, education, and
sport. The treaty was signed in Canberra at 02:00 UTC, the first
stamp on the page's UTC strip. It needs ratification to take effect.

The treaty is the day's most consequential diplomatic story. The
Solomon Islands sit on the sea lanes between Australia and Asia,
and whoever provides their security shapes the region. The 2022
China pact was reported not to be renewed, but the prime minister
would not confirm that, citing a non-disclosure agreement. The desk
prints the treaty and leaves the China pact as reported-not-confirmed,
because that is what the reporting supports.

## The jets

Two F-15EX jets were shown off in public at Kadena, the first
permanent posting of the type outside the United States. Thirty-six
are planned. The jets themselves arrived on 15 November; the public
display was 25 November, which is why the display belongs to this
edition and the arrival does not. The desk's date rule, first report
date counts, is what puts this story on the page.

Kadena is the front line of the Pacific: the largest United States
air base in the region, within range of Taiwan and the South China
Sea. Posting the newest fighter there permanently is a signal, and
the signal is aimed at Beijing. The desk prints the display and
records the arrival date here, so that the date check is transparent.

## The station

Pakistan's Muhammad Zeeshan Ali will become the first foreign
astronaut to visit China's Tiangong space station, joining the
Shenzhou-24 crew. It is a first for the Chinese programme. The story
is labelled REPORTED because no wire carried it in English in the
time available.

The station is the other side of the Pacific shift. While Australia
signs a treaty and the United States posts jets, China flies a
foreign astronaut to its own station. Space is the new theatre of
the competition, and the first foreign guest on Tiangong is a
diplomatic act as much as a scientific one. The desk watches for a
wire report and for the launch date.

## The expulsion

North Korea ordered the Swiss office in Pyongyang shut and its head,
Sylvain Astier, declared persona non grata, to leave within 48
hours, after a row over Swiss sanctions on the Songdowon children's
camp. Kim Yo Jong had threatened closure the day before. The office
had reopened in October, when the foreign minister visited on 30
October.

The expulsion is a small story with a large meaning. Switzerland is
one of the few Western countries with a presence in Pyongyang, and
its office is a channel. Closing it removes a channel. The desk
prints it as a brief because the facts are simple and the meaning is
obvious to anyone who follows the region.

## The pattern

Four stories, one pattern: the Pacific is rearranging itself. A
treaty pulls the Solomon Islands toward Australia. Jets at Kadena
anchor the United States in place. A space station flight pulls
Pakistan toward China. An expulsion in Pyongyang closes a door.
None of these stories mentions the others. Together they describe a
region choosing sides, one signature and one posting at a time.

## What to watch

- Ratification of the Solomon Islands treaty.
- The next F-15EX arrivals at Kadena, and whether the full 36
  arrive on schedule.
- The Shenzhou-24 launch date and the wire confirmation of the
  astronaut story.
- Whether Switzerland reopens a channel to Pyongyang, and through
  whom.

## Why this briefing exists

The page carries four Pacific stories in four different tiers: a
grid item, two briefs, and a feature's neighbour. They do not look
like one story until they are put together. This briefing is where
the Pacific's day is told as one day, and where the pattern the
page only implies is spelled out.

## The week before

The Pacific stories of the 26th did not start on the 26th. The
Solomon Islands treaty was negotiated over months, and the 2022
China pact it is read against was signed four years ago. The F-15EX
jets arrived at Kadena on 15 November, ten days before the public
display. The Shenzhou-24 mission had been scheduled long before the
astronaut's name was announced. The Swiss office in Pyongyang had
reopened only in October, when the foreign minister visited on 30
October, and Kim Yo Jong had threatened closure the day before the
expulsion.

The desk notes this because the page makes the Pacific look like it
moved in a day. It did not move in a day. It moved over a month, and
the 26th is the day the moves became visible. The page records the
day; this briefing records the month.

## How the theme appears on the page

The Pacific is one grid item, the Solomon Islands treaty, one grid
item, the Pakistan astronaut, and two briefs, the fighter jet and
the expulsion. The UTC strip carries the treaty at 02:00, the first
stamp of the day. The map carries the Honiara pin at the far right
edge, the easternmost pin on the page. A reader who reads only the
page will learn that a treaty was signed, that jets were shown, and
that an envoy was expelled. A reader who reads this briefing will
learn that the region is choosing sides, one signature and one
posting at a time.
