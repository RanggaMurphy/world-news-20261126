# Background briefing — The energy shock

How the day's energy stories fit together: a claim in the Gulf, a
price over 94 dollars, mines in the Gulf of Oman, and drones over
Russian refineries. Read this before the dossiers for stories 2,
17, and 18.

## The claim

The day's energy story starts with a claim. Yemen's Houthis said
they struck the Ras Tanura refinery and Riyadh's financial district.
Satellite pictures showed a wide smoke plume. Riyadh said its
defences downed four drones and did not confirm a direct hit. A
coalition strike hit Hodeidah port in response. Brent crude rose
past 94 dollars a barrel, up about 3.5 percent.

The claim matters because Ras Tanura is not a small target. The
refinery's capacity is 550,000 barrels a day, and it sits on the
Gulf coast where the world's oil leaves by tanker. A hit there
would be the largest attack on Saudi energy infrastructure in years.
A claim of a hit there moves the price whether or not the hit
happened, which is why the desk prints the claim as a claim and the
price as a fact.

## The price

Brent at 94.61 dollars is the number the page carries. The rise of
about 3.5 percent is the market's answer to the claim, not to a
confirmed event. Oil traders price risk, and a claim on Ras Tanura
is risk, even unconfirmed. The desk's rule is to print the price
that the market actually reached, because the price is verifiable,
and to label the cause as a claim, because the cause is not.

The price also has a second driver that the page does not have room
to show: the wider pattern of attacks on energy infrastructure
through the year. Ukraine's drones have been hitting Russian
refineries since late October, and refinery runs are down about 45
percent since then. The market reads the Houthi claim against that
background, and the price reflects both.

## The mines

In the same band of hours, the Revolutionary Guard claimed two
tankers hit mines early on Wednesday on an unauthorised route in the
Gulf. The claim is unverified, and the maritime authorities said it
was unconfirmed. The desk prints it as a claim in the briefs, not as
a feature, because there is nothing to confirm: no ship named, no
damage reported, no second source.

The mines story matters even as a claim, because it shows the
pattern of the day: three separate energy incidents, two of them
claims, one of them confirmed. The confirmed one is the Ukrainian
drone campaign. The two claims are the Houthi strike and the mines.
A reader who confuses the three will think the Gulf is on fire. The
page's labels exist to stop that confusion.

## The refineries

Overnight, Ukrainian drones hit the Syzran and Kuibyshev refineries
in Samara and the Novoshakhtinsk refinery in Rostov. One person was
killed in Samara. Seven people were killed in Ukraine by Russian
drones on the same night. Putin had said on 24 November that
retaliation was inevitable.

The refinery campaign is the longest-running energy story of the
autumn. Refinery runs are down about 45 percent since late October.
The strikes are reported by one wire in English, which is why the
story is labelled REPORTED rather than CONFIRMED, but the pattern is
well established: the campaign has been running for weeks, and the
numbers on refinery output come from the industry, not from either
side's claims.

## What to watch

- An independent assessment of damage at Ras Tanura, if any. That
  is the only thing that lifts the Houthi story from CLAIM to
  CONFIRMED.
- The Brent price over the next week. If it holds above 94, the
  market believes the risk. If it falls back, the claim is priced
  as noise.
- The next round of Ukrainian drone strikes and the Russian
  response. The campaign has been escalating since late October,
  and the refinery numbers will show whether it is working.
- Whether the tanker-mine claim is ever confirmed. If no ship is
  named within days, it was probably not a mine strike at all.

## Why this briefing exists

The page has room for a headline and a sentence about energy. The
truth about energy on 26 November 2026 is that it was a day of
claims, and claims need more room than a headline gives them. This
briefing is that room. It does not change any label on the page. It
explains why the labels are what they are.

## The week before

The energy stories of the 26th did not start on the 26th. The
Ukrainian drone campaign against Russian refineries has been
running since late October, and refinery runs are down about 45
percent since then. The Houthi campaign against shipping and Saudi
targets has been running far longer, and the coalition strike on
Hodeidah port that followed the Ras Tanura claim is part of a
pattern of retaliation, not a new departure. The Brent price had
been climbing before the claim, and the claim accelerated a move
that was already under way.

The desk notes this because a reader who sees only the 26th will
think the day was exceptional. It was not exceptional. It was a
day in a campaign, and the campaign is the story. The page records
the day; this briefing records the campaign.

## How the theme appears on the page

Energy is the second feature, the Houthi claim, and two briefs,
the tanker mines and the refinery strikes. The numbers strip
carries Brent at 94 dollars and over. The UTC strip carries the
claim at 05:00. The map carries the Ras Tanura pin. A reader who
reads only the page will learn that a claim was made, that oil
rose, and that two more incidents happened. A reader who reads this
briefing will learn which of those three are claims and which one
is confirmed, and why the distinction matters more than the price.
